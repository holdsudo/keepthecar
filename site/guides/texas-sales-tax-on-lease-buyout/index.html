Texas Sales Tax on a Lease Buyout: The Comptroller's Second-Transaction Rule

In Texas, the tax you paid when the lessor first registered the car does not follow you into the buyout. The Comptroller calls the purchase a second transaction.

Published October 26, 2026 · bestleaseend.com

Two transactions, not one

The Texas Comptroller's Motor Vehicle Tax Guide separates leases into types. For an operating lease, the usual consumer lease, the guide says that buying the vehicle at the end is a new taxable sale, a second transaction. The tax is based on the amount paid at the conclusion of the lease.

The rate and the base

The Comptroller's guide refers to the 6.25 percent motor vehicle tax. For a purchase through a licensed Texas dealer, the base is the sales price less any trade-in allowance and dealer discounts. For a buyout, the amount you pay to take the car is what matters. Illustration only: a $22,000 buyout price at 6.25 percent is $1,375 of tax.

What you can and cannot get credit for

Tax paidCredit against your buyout tax?
Tax the lessor paid when it first bought and titled the carNo. The lessee "cannot claim a credit for tax paid in the lessor's name"
Use tax or new-resident tax you paid personally on a car you brought into TexasYes, the guide allows a credit

For the credit that is allowed, the guide says the Texas tax, title, and registration receipt is the only acceptable proof of Texas tax paid. Keep it.

The exception: a conditional sale

Some contracts are called leases but are treated as sales from the start. The guide says that for a conditional sale, one taxable sale has occurred, and if the lessee later takes title, tax is recalculated on total consideration. The distinction turns on your contract. If you are not sure which kind you signed, ask the Comptroller or a tax professional before you assume either result.

Read the second page of the contract. The words "lease" or "purchase option" on the front do not decide how Texas taxes it. The terms do.

What to do with this

  1. Get the buyout price in writing.
  2. Multiply by 6.25 percent to estimate the tax, then add title and registration fees.
  3. Do not subtract tax the lessor paid. Subtract only what you personally paid and can document.
  4. If your lender finances the buyout, ask whether the tax can be included in the loan.

If you leased outside Texas and moved here

The Comptroller's guide on credits has a section for leased vehicles. It says a Texas resident, or someone domiciled or doing business in Texas, can claim credit for tax paid on a vehicle leased outside Texas and brought in for use. Either the lessor or the lessee may have paid the tax, and either can document it. Monthly lease payments count if they were made before the vehicle entered Texas. Tax not yet paid at registration cannot be credited, but the lessee may request a refund from the other state of any additional tax paid there at lease end.

The credit is dollar-for-dollar and covers similar sales or use tax paid to another state or a local government in it. The county tax assessor-collector must see a receipt, invoice or other document showing the amount of tax paid and the owner's name, so gather that paperwork early.

Standard presumptive value

The Comptroller's guide notes that standard presumptive value, or SPV, procedures may also apply to a lessee's purchase. For private-party and out-of-state purchases, the guide says the price paid is compared with a percentage of the vehicle's SPV or a certified appraisal. In practice, that means a buyout priced far below a car's book-style value can be looked at more closely than one priced near it. Ask the county tax office how it will treat your price.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Does the quote show sales tax, or only the price? Who collects the tax and when?
  • Does the contract fit the operating-lease description or the conditional-sale one? Ask the Comptroller if the contract wording is unclear.
  • If I paid use tax or new-resident tax on this car myself, do I have the Texas receipt that proves it?
  • Can my loan cover the tax and fees together?

Put the answers in writing before you commit, because a tax figure agreed on the phone can differ from the one on the paperwork.

What this guide does not settle

The Comptroller's guide describes the general rules. It does not decide how your contract is classified, and individual rulings can turn on contract details. Rates, credits and procedures can change, so check the linked sources for current text. This is general information, not tax advice.

The short version

For a standard Texas operating lease, the Comptroller treats a buyout as a second taxable sale at 6.25 percent of what you pay. The lessor's original tax is not a credit for you. Check your contract for the conditional-sale exception.

Quick answers

Is buying out a lease taxable in Texas?

Yes in the usual case. The Comptroller's Motor Vehicle Tax Guide says that when a lessee buys the vehicle at the end of an operating lease, it is a new taxable sale, a second transaction, and the tax is based on the amount paid at the conclusion of the lease.

What is the Texas motor vehicle tax rate?

The Comptroller's guide refers to the 6.25 percent motor vehicle tax.

Do I get credit for the tax the lessor paid?

No. The guide says the lessee cannot claim a credit for tax paid in the lessor's name. Credit is allowed for use tax or new-resident tax the lessee paid personally on a vehicle brought into Texas.

What if my lease was really a conditional sale?

The guide describes a conditional sale as one taxable sale from the start, with tax recalculated on total consideration when the lessee takes title. Whether your contract is one depends on its terms, so ask the Comptroller or a tax professional.
