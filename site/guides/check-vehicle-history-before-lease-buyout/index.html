Run a Vehicle History Report Before You Buy Out Your Lease

You have lived with the car for three years, so you know how it drives. The title record is a different question, and it is free to look up before you put money on it.

Published October 23, 2026 · bestleaseend.com

Why look at a car you already drive

Most buyout advice is about the money: the residual, the fees, the tax. All of that assumes the car in your driveway is the car the paperwork describes. A vehicle history report is the quick way to check that assumption before you commit to a loan.

It is not about finding out whether the car drives well. You know that. It is about three narrower things: whether the title carries a brand you did not know about, whether the odometer record lines up with the number on your dash, and what the car's record will look like to the next person who looks it up when you sell it.

The federal database behind the reports

The National Motor Vehicle Title Information System, or NMVTIS, is a federal database that pulls title data from state motor-vehicle agencies, insurers and salvage operations. The Justice Department's Bureau of Justice Assistance runs the consumer site, VehicleHistory.gov, which lists the approved providers that sell NMVTIS reports.

According to that site, a report covers the current and historical state of title, the title issue date, brand history, the odometer reading, and total-loss and salvage history. A brand is a label a state puts on a title, such as salvage, junk or flood. The system also keeps a history of brands applied to a vehicle by any state, which is the point: a brand removed in one state can still show up in the national record.

What a clean report does not prove

QuestionNMVTIS answers it?
Has the title ever been branded salvage, junk or flood?Yes, if the states reported it
Was the car ever declared a total loss?Yes, where reported
Does the odometer history look consistent?Yes, from title records
Was it in a fender-bender that nobody reported?No
Has it been repaired, and by whom?No. NMVTIS holds no repair history

The FTC's used-car advice makes the same point: reports from other providers sometimes carry accident and repair history that the federal system does not. A report can also come back empty, which usually means no participating state has reported anything on that VIN. Empty is not the same as spotless.

How to run one

  1. Find your VIN on the lower-left corner of the windshield, the driver's door jamb, or your registration.
  2. Go to VehicleHistory.gov and pick from the approved providers it lists. Check the list when you go, because the page itself warns that availability changes by provider.
  3. Read the title section first, then the odometer entries, then any total-loss or salvage records.
  4. Compare the most recent odometer entry to your dash. A big gap is normal if the last record is old. A reading higher than your dash is not.
  5. Save a copy with your buyout papers.

What to do with a surprise. If the report shows a brand or a total-loss record you were never told about, stop before you sign a loan. Call the leasing company and ask them to explain it in writing. It changes what the car is worth, which changes whether the payoff is a good price.

Why this matters even when the car is fine

Your equity estimate assumes a clean history. Online value tools price a car on mileage, trim and condition, not on what is buried in the title record. If you plan to keep the car for years, you will still sell it eventually, and the person who looks it up then will see everything on the record, including anything you did not catch now.

It also helps with your lender and your state. A title that moves from the leasing company to you is simpler when the record is clean, and a brand you did not know about is far better discovered before the paperwork starts than in the middle of it.

The short version

Run the VIN through an approved provider on VehicleHistory.gov before you finance a buyout. A federal report shows title brands, total-loss and salvage records, and odometer history. It does not show repairs, so treat a clean result as good news, not proof.

Quick answers

Do I need a vehicle history report if I leased the car from new?

It is not required to buy out a lease, but it is cheap insurance. You know how the car has been driven, not what any database says about its title, total-loss history or odometer records. A report also shows you what a future buyer will see.

What does a federal NMVTIS report show?

According to the Justice Department's VehicleHistory.gov site, an NMVTIS report covers the current and historical state of title, the title issue date, brand history, the odometer reading, and any total-loss and salvage history.

Does an NMVTIS report include accident or repair history?

No. NMVTIS does not contain repair history. The FTC notes that reports from other providers sometimes add accident and repair information, so a clean title record does not prove a car was never damaged.
