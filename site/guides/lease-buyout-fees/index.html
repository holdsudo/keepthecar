Lease Buyout Fees: Which Are Real and Which Are Padding

A lease buyout has two legitimate costs on top of the residual: the fee your contract names and the tax and title charges your state collects. Everything else on the sheet is somebody's margin.

Published October 19, 2026 · bestleaseend.com

Start with the only number that matters

Your buyout starts with the residual value printed in your lease. That number was set the day you signed and it is the price the leasing company agreed to sell you the car for. Everything in this guide is about what gets stacked on top of it, and by whom.

The honest way to think about lease buyout fees is to sort them into three piles: fees your contract names, fees your state charges, and fees somebody made up in the last ten minutes. The first two are real. The third pile is where the money leaks.

Purchase-option fee

Most leases name a purchase-option fee, a few hundred dollars, that the leasing company charges when you exercise the option to buy. It is in the contract you signed, so it is real. It is also the one fee the leasing company's own payoff letter will show you, which makes the payoff letter the cleanest document in this whole process. Get it directly from the leasing company, not from the dealer.

Sales tax

The tax you paid during the lease covered the lease payments. A buyout is a new sale, so most states tax the buyout price again, at the rate where the car is registered. This is real, it is usually the biggest line after the residual, and nobody at a dealer or a bank will finance it for you unless you ask. A buyout partner that only lends you the residual has left you to find the tax in cash.

Title and registration

The title moves from the leasing company's name to yours, or to your new lender's. The state charges for the title, the lien recording, and new registration. These are real and small. The dealer will charge you for doing them. A specialist does them as part of the job.

Disposition fee

The disposition fee is what the leasing company charges to take the car back and remarket it. It is a return fee. If you are buying the car, there is nothing to dispose of, and your contract almost certainly says the fee is waived on a purchase. If a disposition fee appears on a buyout quote, somebody copied the wrong column. Point at the contract and make them remove it.

Doc fee

This is the dealer charging you for paperwork. The leasing company already generated the buyout package. The state already has the title forms. The dealer's doc fee pays for the dealer's time typing your name into a buyer's order, and in most states it is uncapped and runs into the hundreds. It exists because the dealer did not want this sale and intends to be paid for it anyway. The way to avoid a doc fee is to not do the buyout through a dealer.

Inspection fee

A lease-end inspection exists to bill you for wear and damage before the car goes back to the leasing company. If you are keeping the car, the wear is yours and nobody needs to inspect it. A dealer that charges you to inspect a car you are buying as-is has invented a service. Decline it.

Processing or service fee

Big online buyout outfits charge a flat fee for the convenience of a website. Sometimes it is disclosed on page one, sometimes it appears on the closing documents. Either way it is usually rolled into the loan, so you pay interest on the fee for the life of the loan. Ask for the fee in writing before you apply, and ask whether it is financed.

Early termination charges

If you buy out before the lease ends, the leasing company adds the remaining payments, sometimes discounted, sometimes not, plus any early termination charge the contract names. These are real, and they are the reason an early buyout is almost always more expensive than waiting for the last month.

The one-line test. If a fee is not in your lease contract and not on your state's DMV fee schedule, it is negotiable, and it is usually removable by walking away from whoever added it.

Why the dealer's quote is always higher

Ask the leasing company for your payoff and you get one number: residual plus purchase-option fee, with a date it expires. Ask the dealer for "the buyout" and you get a different, larger number. The gap is the doc fee, the inspection, any add-on the finance office can attach, and, if the dealer arranges your loan, a rate with their compensation built in. None of that is your buyout. It is the dealer's buyout of your patience.

The dealer will also tell you the leasing company requires the buyout to go through them. Sometimes a leasing company does require a dealer for a third-party buyer. For the lessee buying their own car, the payoff letter from the leasing company is the rule, and the letter will say who it can be paid by.

What to do with this

  1. Call the leasing company, not the dealer, and get the payoff letter. Note the expiration date.
  2. Add your state's sales tax on the buyout price and the DMV title and registration charges. That is your real number.
  3. Any quote above that number is somebody's fee. Ask which contract line or state schedule it comes from.
  4. If the answer is a shrug, that fee is optional, and so is that vendor.

The short version

Two fees are real on a lease buyout: the purchase-option fee in your contract and your state's tax and title charges. The disposition fee is a return fee and does not belong on a purchase.

Doc fees, inspection fees and processing fees are not part of the buyout. They are the price of using a dealer or a volume website, and the way to avoid them is to use neither.

Quick answers

Do I pay the disposition fee if I buy out my lease?

Almost never. The disposition fee covers returning the car to the leasing company. Most contracts waive it on a purchase. If it appears on a buyout quote, ask them to show you the contract line and have it removed.

Can the dealer charge a doc fee on a lease buyout?

If you do the buyout through the dealer, they will. The leasing company's payoff letter does not include one. Doing the buyout directly with the leasing company, or through a specialist that does not charge one, is how you avoid it.

Is sales tax really charged again on a lease buyout?

In most states, yes. The tax during the lease applied to the payments. The buyout is a separate purchase and is taxed on the buyout price at the rate where the car is registered. Check your state's DMV or tax site for the rate.
