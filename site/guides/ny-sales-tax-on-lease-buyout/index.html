Taxes: New York

New York Sales Tax on a Lease Buyout: Yes, You Pay It Again

The sales tax you paid during the lease was tax on the payments. The buyout is a new sale, and New York taxes it again, on the full buyout price, at the rate where the car is registered.

Published October 19, 2026 · bestleaseend.com

Why you pay sales tax twice on the same car

For three years the leasing company owned your car and rented it to you. New York taxed the rent. Every monthly payment carried sales tax, and if your lease was structured with the tax paid up front, that lump sum covered the payments too. None of it was tax on the car itself. It was tax on the right to drive it.

When you buy the car at the end of the lease, the leasing company sells it to you. That is a retail sale of a used vehicle, and New York taxes retail sales of vehicles. The tax you already paid does not carry over, does not get credited, and does not reduce the new bill. If you searched for NY tax on lease buyout hoping the answer was no, this is the answer. It is yes, on the full buyout price.

The rate: 4% state plus wherever the car sleeps

New York's state sales tax is 4%. On top of it sits a local rate set by the county, or by New York City, and the local rate that applies is the one where the car is registered. Not where the dealer is. Not where the leasing company mails its letters from. Where you live and where the plates go.

In New York City the combined rate is 8.875%. The Long Island counties run higher than most of upstate. Upstate counties vary, and two people with the same car and the same residual can owe different amounts because one lives across a county line from the other.

Confirm the number before you use it

Local rates change. The combined rate for your county or city is published by the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance, and that is the only figure to build your buyout on. This guide gives you the mechanics, not a rate table. Look up your own rate, by your registered address, on the Tax Department's site before you do any math.

What it does to the buyout number

Here is the shape of it, with a hypothetical car. Say the residual in your lease is $20,000 and the car is registered in New York City.

That $1,775 is not in the payoff letter. The leasing company quotes you the residual plus its purchase-option fee and leaves the state out of it, because the state is not its problem. It is yours, at the DMV window, in cash, unless you planned for it. The full walk-through of building that number is in how to calculate a lease buyout.

Move the same car to a Long Island county and the tax line goes up. Move it to a lower-rate county upstate and it comes down. Same car, same contract, different bill. The registered address decides.

Who collects it, and when

If you do the buyout yourself, the DMV collects the tax when you title and register the car in your name. You bring the title paperwork from the leasing company, the sales tax form, and the money. No tax paid, no title, no plates.

If a dealer is in the middle of the deal, the dealer collects the tax on the buyer's order and remits it to the state. The amount is the same. The difference is everything else on that buyer's order: the doc fee, the inspection nobody needed, the add-ons. The tax is the one line the dealer did not invent. The lines around it are covered in lease buyout fees.

A buyout partner that does these every day pays the tax to the DMV as part of titling the car, and rolls it into the loan. You never stand at the window.

Can you register it somewhere cheaper?

No. The car is registered at the address where you live, and the tax follows the registration. Registering a car at a relative's address upstate to save on the rate is not a strategy. It is a false statement to the DMV. Pay your county's rate.

The rule. New York sales tax on a lease buyout is charged on the buyout price, at the combined rate for your registered address, and paid when the car is titled. Nothing you paid during the lease counts toward it.

Why financing the tax matters

On a $20,000 residual in the city, the tax is close to two thousand dollars. That is the number that turns a buyout from "sign and drive" into "find cash this week." The payoff letter expires in days, and the tax is due the day the car is titled.

This is where the lender you chose shows its hand. A big bank or a credit union writes a loan against a book value and tells you the tax is yours. A dealer will finance it, with its own compensation built into the rate. A specialist finances the residual, the purchase-option fee and the tax as one loan, pays the state, and hands you the plates. The homepage lays out who refuses to finance what.

Before anyone pulls your credit, ask one question in writing: is the New York sales tax included in the loan amount? If the answer is anything other than yes, you are about to pay it in cash.

What to do with this

  1. Get the payoff letter from the leasing company, not the dealer. Note the expiration date.
  2. Look up the combined sales tax rate for your registered address on the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance site.
  3. Multiply the buyout price by that rate. Add the DMV title and registration charges. That is your real number.
  4. Decide, before the letter expires, whether the tax comes out of savings or goes into the loan. Get the answer in writing from whoever is lending.
  5. If a dealer's quote is higher than residual plus purchase-option fee plus tax plus DMV charges, ask which contract line or state schedule the difference comes from.

The short version

Yes, New York charges sales tax on a lease buyout, and yes, you already paid sales tax during the lease. Those are two different taxes on two different transactions. The buyout is taxed at 4% state plus the local rate for your registered address, 8.875% in New York City, and the money is due when the car is titled.

The tax is real and it is not negotiable. What is negotiable is who finances it. Confirm your rate with the state, build it into your number, and do not sign with anyone who leaves it out.

Quick answers

Do I pay sales tax again if I buy out my lease in New York?

Yes. The tax during the lease applied to the lease payments. The buyout is a separate sale of the car to you, and New York taxes it on the buyout price at the combined rate for the address where the car is registered.

Is the lease buyout tax higher in NYC than upstate?

Usually. New York City's combined rate is 8.875%, and the Long Island counties sit above most upstate counties. The rate is set by where the car is registered, so confirm your own county's figure on the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance site.

Can I finance the New York sales tax on a lease buyout?

It depends on the lender. Big banks and credit unions commonly lend against a book value and leave the tax to you. A specialist finances the residual, the fees and the tax as one loan and pays the DMV directly. Ask in writing before you apply.
