Early Lease Buyout: How Early, What It Costs, When It's Dumb

An early buyout is the rest of your lease paid in one check. It is rarely a bargain, sometimes the right move, and always a number you should get from the leasing company, not the dealer.

Published October 19, 2026 · bestleaseend.com

How early can you buy out a lease?

The search phrase how to buyout lease early has a boring answer: call the leasing company and ask for an early payoff quote. The more useful question is whether you should.

Whether you can at all depends on your contract. Many leases allow a purchase at any point in the term. Some restrict it during the first months, and some describe the price differently depending on when you ask. The section headed purchase option or early termination says which applies to you. Read it before you call, so you know whether the number you get back matches the method the contract promised.

Someone searching kia lease early buyout, or the same question for any brand, will get one answer from the dealer and another from the brand's finance arm. The finance arm holds the lease. Its payoff letter is the number. Ask it for the current rule in writing, and treat anything the dealer says as the dealer's preference until the letter agrees.

What an early payoff is made of

At the end of the term, your buyout is the residual plus the purchase-option fee, plus your state's tax and title. Early, it is all of that plus the payments you have not made yet. The calculation guide covers the full formula. The early-only pieces are these.

  • Remaining payments. Every payment left on the schedule. Some leasing companies reduce this to remove finance charges that have not accrued yet, using the method named in the early termination section. Some do not reduce it. The payoff letter shows which.
  • Early termination charge. If the contract names one for a purchase before term end, it applies. Many contracts apply it only to returns, not purchases. Read the paragraph.
  • Tax on the larger amount. Sales tax is charged on what you pay. Ask your state's tax or motor vehicle agency how an early buyout is figured, because the remaining payments may or may not be part of the taxable price where you live.

A hypothetical

Say the residual is $18,000 and the purchase-option fee is $300. Say you have ten payments of $400 left, and the leasing company does not discount them. Your early payoff is $18,300 plus $4,000, or $22,300, before tax and title. If you waited ten months instead, you would pay the same $4,000 in monthly installments and then $18,300 at the end. Same total. The early buyout did not make the car cheaper. It made the payments due today.

Every number in that paragraph is made up. The structure is what matters: an early buyout, undiscounted, is a prepayment, not a discount.

Why it usually costs more than waiting

Three reasons, and they stack.

  1. You borrow to prepay. If you take a loan to buy early, you are paying interest on money that was going to be paid monthly, interest-free, inside the lease payment. If the leasing company does not discount the remaining payments, you have paid the lease's finance charge and your new loan's interest on the same months.
  2. You give up the walk-away. Until the term ends, you hold an option: if the market drops below the residual, you return the car and the leasing company eats the loss. Buying early cancels that option. Months later, the car might be worth less than you paid.
  3. Tax lands now, on a bigger number. If your state taxes the whole early payoff, you are paying tax on the remaining payments as part of a purchase price. Confirm this with the state before you decide; it can be the difference between a sensible buyout and a silly one.

Add the usual fees on top and the gap widens. None of this makes an early buyout wrong. It makes it something to do for a reason, not out of impatience.

The equity spike is the common one. If the market has moved far above your residual, the gap is yours only while it exists. Whether that justifies buying now is the subject of should I buy out my lease, and it comes down to whether you would keep the car and whether the loan is decent.

The one-line rule. Buy early for a reason you can name in one sentence. If the sentence starts with "the dealer said," wait.

How to get an early payoff quote

  1. Call the leasing company, not the dealer. Ask for the payoff department.
  2. Ask for the lessee payoff, in writing, with the good-through date and the per-day charge after it.
  3. Ask whether the remaining payments are discounted and by what method. Ask whether the purchase-option fee and any early termination charge are included.
  4. Ask whether a third-party payoff is a different amount, if you might sell rather than keep.
  5. Get the sales tax treatment from your state's tax or motor vehicle agency, not from the leasing company.
  6. Add it up, compare it to the car's private-party value, and compare it to what waiting costs.

When it is dumb

Three cases where an early buyout is just a worse purchase.

Negative equity. The car is worth less than the early payoff. You would be paying today for the right to overpay. Keep driving and decide at term end, when the walk-away is still yours.

A bad loan to feel like an owner. If the only money available is at an ugly rate, you are trading an interest-free prepayment schedule for an expensive one. Wait, improve the rate, or wait for the term.

The dealer's early buyout. The dealer calls, says your car is in demand, and offers to "buy you out early" into a new lease. What happens is the dealer collects your equity and starts your payments over. If your car is worth that much, it is worth that much to you. The four traps explain why the dealer wanted that call.

The short version

An early lease buyout is the rest of the lease paid now, plus the usual fees and tax on a larger number. Undiscounted, it is a prepayment, not a saving.

Do it for a reason: an equity spike you would otherwise lose, a mileage bill you can avoid, a move the lease cannot follow. Get the quote from the leasing company in writing, check the tax with the state, and never let the dealer be the one to suggest it.

Quick answers

How early can you buy out a lease?

It depends on your contract. Many leases allow a purchase at any time, some restrict the first months. Read the purchase option and early termination sections, then ask the leasing company for an early payoff in writing.

Is an early lease buyout cheaper than waiting?

Usually not. The early payoff adds every remaining payment to the residual, sometimes without a discount, and you may pay tax on the larger amount. It is a prepayment, and it only wins when equity, mileage or a move makes waiting cost more.

Can I buy out a Kia lease early?

Ask the brand's finance arm, which holds the lease, for its current rule and an early payoff quote in writing. The dealer's answer is not the rule. The contract's purchase option section tells you what the quote should contain.
