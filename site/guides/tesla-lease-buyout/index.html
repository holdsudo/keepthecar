Tesla Lease Buyout: What Your Agreement Says Beats What the Forum Says

Every Tesla lease buyout thread is a pile of answers that were true for somebody else's agreement in some other year. Yours has one source of truth, and it is not a forum.

Published October 19, 2026 · bestleaseend.com

Why the forum is wrong even when it is right

Tesla has changed whether, and how, a lessee can buy the car at the end of the lease more than once. People who leased in different years signed different agreements. So a thread full of confident answers is really a thread full of people describing their own contract, and none of them signed yours.

That is the whole problem with a Tesla lease buyout in one sentence: the answer depends on the paper you signed and on what Tesla's lease-end process says today, and both of those are things you can check in a few minutes without asking a stranger. This guide will not tell you what Tesla's current rule is, because any rule printed here would be stale the day Tesla changed it. It will tell you where the rule lives and what to do with it.

Where the real answer lives

Your lease agreement

Open the lease agreement you signed, the actual document, not a summary. Find the section on end-of-term options. It will say one of a few things: that you may purchase the vehicle for the residual value plus a purchase-option fee, that you may not purchase it, or that purchase is at the lessor's discretion. It will also state the residual. That residual is a contract number set on the day you signed, and leasing companies rarely move it. If the agreement says you may purchase, that is your right, in writing, regardless of what anyone online says.

The Tesla app and account

Tesla handles the lease-end process through the app and your online account rather than through a dealer, because there is no dealer. Look for the lease-end or end-of-term section in the app as the final months approach. If a purchase is offered, the app is where the payoff quote and the steps to complete it appear. If no purchase option is shown and your agreement says you may purchase, contact Tesla through the account and ask, in writing, how to exercise the option your agreement grants. Keep the exchange.

What to ask, in writing

  • Does my agreement permit a lessee purchase at the end of the term?
  • What is the payoff quote and when does it expire?
  • Does the quote include the purchase-option fee, and is the disposition fee waived on a purchase?
  • Can an outside lender pay the payoff on my behalf, and what does the lender need to send?
  • How is the title or lien release delivered after payoff, to whom, and how long does it take?

The general shape of a buyout, which is the same for a Tesla as for any other car once a purchase is allowed, is in how a lease buyout works.

What a buyout looks like if your agreement allows one

Take a hypothetical Tesla Model 3 lease buyout. Say the residual in the agreement is $24,000 and the agreement names a purchase-option fee of $350. The payoff quote is the sum of those plus any remaining payments if you go before the last month. That quote is good until the date on it, which is usually days away, not weeks.

Two things stand out. First, the dealer column is empty, which is the one real advantage of a direct-sales brand: the doc fee and the finance-office add-ons that pad every other buyout have no building to live in. Second, sales tax is the biggest line after the residual and it is paid at title, in your state, at your state's rate. Most states tax the buyout as a new purchase. Find the rate on your state agency's site and budget for it, because a loan that covers only the residual leaves you finding the tax in cash.

The title then moves from Tesla's lessor entity to you, or to your lender with a lien recorded. That step, including the odometer statement and the lien release, is walked through in the title transfer guide.

Should you, even if you can

A buyout is only worth doing if the residual is at or below what the car is worth to you. Electric vehicle prices have moved sharply in both directions, and a residual set years ago can be well above or well below the market today. Price the car before you decide anything. If the market is below the residual, the lease was the insurance and returning the car is the payout. If the market is above the residual, the equity is yours and returning the car hands it back for nothing. The decision framework is in should I buy out my lease.

The one-line rule. Your signed agreement and the Tesla app are the only two sources for a Tesla lease buyout. Anyone else is describing a different contract.

The trap list

  • Acting on a forum post. It describes someone else's agreement from some other year. Read yours.
  • Waiting for the app to offer it. If your agreement grants a purchase option and the app does not show one, ask in writing. Do not let the term end while you wait.
  • Letting the payoff quote expire. Line up the money first, then pull the quote, then send funds before the date on it.
  • Financing only the residual. Tax and title are real and due at the DMV. A lender that caps the loan at book value or refuses the tax leaves you with a cash gap.
  • Big online lease-end outfits. They will happily take a Tesla file, charge a processing fee, roll it into the loan, and stretch the term. The margin is in the fee line. Read it first. The pattern is described in the four traps on the homepage.
  • Skipping the market check. A residual is not a price. It is the number in the contract. Whether it is a good price is a separate question that only the current market answers.
  • Ignoring the mileage and wear terms. If you return the car, the excess mileage and wear charges apply. If you buy it, they do not. That difference is sometimes the whole argument for buying.

The last sixty days

  1. Read the end-of-term section of your agreement. Note the residual, the purchase-option fee, and whether purchase is permitted.
  2. Check the lease-end section of the Tesla app and account. Screenshot what it offers.
  3. Price the car at market and decide: keep, buy and sell, or return.
  4. If buying, ask Tesla in writing the five questions above, and save the replies.
  5. Add your state's sales tax and DMV charges, confirmed with the state agency, to the payoff. Arrange financing for that whole number.
  6. Pull a fresh payoff quote, send funds before it expires, and track the title until it arrives.

The short version

Whether you can buy out your Tesla lease is written in your agreement, and how to do it is shown in the app. Neither of those lives on a forum, and this guide will not pretend to know the rule for your contract.

If a purchase is allowed, the buyout is the residual plus the purchase-option fee plus your state's tax and title, with no dealer in the way. Check the market before you decide, get every answer from Tesla in writing, and finance the whole number, not just the residual.

Quick answers

Can I buy out my Tesla lease?

It depends on the agreement you signed and on Tesla's current lease-end process, both of which have changed over time. Read the end-of-term section of your own agreement and check the lease-end section of the Tesla app. If they disagree, ask Tesla in writing.

How much does a Tesla Model 3 lease buyout cost?

The residual value in your agreement, plus the purchase-option fee if one is named, plus your state's sales tax on the buyout price and the DMV title and registration charges. There is no dealer doc fee because there is no dealer.

Why does the forum say something different from my agreement?

Because the people posting signed different agreements in different years. Tesla has changed its lease-end terms more than once. Your agreement is the only one that applies to your car.
