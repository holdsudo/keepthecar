Pennsylvania Sales Tax on a Lease Buyout: What the Regulation Says

Buying out a Pennsylvania lease is a taxable purchase. The regulation says so in a sentence, and it is worth reading before you budget.

Published October 26, 2026 · bestleaseend.com

The rule in one sentence

Pennsylvania's regulation on vehicle leases, 61 Pa. Code § 47.17, covers leases that include an option to buy. For those, it says sales tax applies to the lease payments and to any payment made in exercising the option to buy. If your contract gives you the right to buy the car at lease end and you use it, the price you pay is a taxable payment.

The rate

The Pennsylvania Department of Revenue lists the state sales tax at 6 percent. Allegheny County adds a local 1 percent and Philadelphia adds a local 2 percent. Which rate applies to you depends on where you are, so confirm it with the Department of Revenue or at the time you title the car.

WhereCombined rate (state + local add-on)
Most of Pennsylvania6%
Allegheny County7% (6% + 1%)
Philadelphia8% (6% + 2%)

An illustration

This is arithmetic, not a quote. If a buyout price were $20,000 and the 6 percent rate applied, the tax would be $1,200. In Philadelphia at 8 percent it would be $1,600. Tax is on top of the price, so the number to finance or save for is the price plus tax plus title and registration fees.

What about tax you already paid on the lease?

The regulation taxes the lease payments and the option payment as two separate things. We did not find a provision that credits what you paid on lease payments against the buyout. The credit the regulation does describe, in subsection (e), is for vehicles purchased or leased for use outside Pennsylvania: it equals tax paid to another state, and only if that state gives substantially similar relief. It also says a lessor's tax on its own purchase of the vehicle cannot be credited against what the lessee owes on lease payments.

Do not assume a credit. If a dealer or lender tells you the lease tax comes off the buyout, ask them to show you the rule. If you cannot find one, build your numbers without it, and treat any credit as a pleasant surprise.

Where the tax is collected

Your payoff quote or purchase agreement should show whether tax is included. If it is not, expect it to be collected when the car is titled. Ask the leasing company, and ask your lender whether the loan can include the tax so it is not due out of pocket.

If you leased in another state

The same regulation sorts lease payments by where the lease was signed and where the car is used. For a lease executed in Pennsylvania, all lease payments are taxable. For a lease executed outside Pennsylvania, a resident lessee's payments are taxable if the vehicle is brought into Pennsylvania during the corresponding lease period, unless an exemption in the regulation applies. A nonresident lessee's payments are taxable if the vehicle is used in Pennsylvania for more than seven days in the corresponding period, and a tourist or vacationer is not taxed.

That language is about lease payments. It does not walk through a person who leased a car elsewhere, moved to Pennsylvania, and then bought it out. If that is you, ask the Department of Revenue how the purchase will be treated and what proof of any tax paid to the other state it will want. Do not assume the answer from a neighboring state's rules.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Does the payoff quote include sales tax, or is it only the price? If it does not, who collects the tax and when?
  • Which rate is being applied, and based on what address?
  • Is the option price the only taxable payment, or are other charges, such as a purchase option fee, added to the base?
  • Can my loan include the tax and the title fees, so nothing is due out of pocket at the counter?
  • If a credit is being offered, which rule does it come from?

Get the answers in writing. A tax number that exists only on the phone can change by the time you sign.

What this guide does not settle

The regulation tells you that the option payment is taxable. It does not give a line-by-line list of every fee that may be inside a payoff, and it cannot tell you how your particular contract is drafted. Rates and rules also change, and the sources linked below are the place to check the current text. This is general information, not tax advice.

The short version

In Pennsylvania the payment that exercises a lease purchase option is taxable under 61 Pa. Code § 47.17. The state rate is 6 percent, plus local add-ons in Allegheny County and Philadelphia. Budget for the tax on the full buyout price, and do not count on a credit for lease-payment tax unless someone can show you the rule.

Quick answers

Is a lease buyout taxable in Pennsylvania?

The Pennsylvania Code says that when a vehicle lease includes an option to buy, sales tax applies to the lease payments and to any payment made in exercising the option to buy. A buyout under that option is therefore taxable.

What is the Pennsylvania sales tax rate?

The Department of Revenue lists a 6 percent state rate, with Allegheny County adding 1 percent and Philadelphia adding 2 percent.

Do I get credit for the tax I paid on my lease payments?

We did not find a provision in 61 Pa. Code § 47.17 that credits lease-payment tax against the purchase. The credit it describes is for tax paid to another state on vehicles used outside Pennsylvania. Ask the Department of Revenue or a tax professional about your case.
