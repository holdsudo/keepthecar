Maryland Lease Buyout Tax: The 6% Excise Tax, Explained

Maryland does not call it sales tax, and that confuses people into thinking there is none. There is a 6% vehicle excise tax, collected by the MVA when the car is titled, and a lease buyout is a title transfer.

Published October 19, 2026 · bestleaseend.com

Maryland does not charge sales tax on cars. It charges this instead.

Search for lease buyout sales tax Maryland and the first thing to learn is that the state does not have one. Vehicles are carved out of Maryland's sales tax. In its place the state charges a vehicle excise tax, sometimes called the titling tax, and the Motor Vehicle Administration collects it whenever a vehicle is titled to a new owner.

The rate is 6% of the purchase price. The money goes to the MVA at the moment the title moves into your name. If you are buying your leased car, the title moves from the leasing company to you, and the MVA assesses the excise tax on the price you paid for it. A different name on the form does not make it a smaller bill.

A lease buyout is a purchase

For the length of the lease the leasing company owned the car and you paid to use it. At the end, the leasing company sells it to you for the residual value in the contract plus whatever purchase-option fee the contract names. That is a purchase. Maryland taxes purchases of vehicles through the excise tax, so the buyout is taxed.

Whatever was collected at the start of or during the lease was collected on the lease. It was tied to the leasing company's ownership and your use. The buyout creates a new title in your name, and the MVA assesses excise tax on that new title. There is no credit from the lease period to apply against it. If somebody at a dealership tells you the tax was "already taken care of," ask them to show you where on the MVA's paperwork that credit appears.

The excise tax is the largest line after the residual on almost every buyout, and it is the one line the payoff letter never shows. The leasing company quotes its number and leaves the state out of it. You add the state back in, and the full method is in how to calculate a lease buyout.

What "purchase price" means here

The MVA assesses the tax on the price of the purchase. On a buyout that is the buyout price you pay the leasing company. How the MVA treats the purchase-option fee, and whether it ever looks at a book value instead of the contract price, is a question for the MVA, not for this guide and not for the dealer. Ask the MVA directly and get the answer before you build your number.

Who collects it, and when

Do the buyout yourself and you pay the excise tax at the MVA when you apply for the title. You bring the title from the leasing company, the lien release, the buyout paperwork showing the price, and the money. No tax, no title, no tags.

Go through a dealer and the dealer collects the excise tax on the buyer's order and remits it to the MVA with the title work. The tax is the same. What is not the same is the rest of the buyer's order. Doc fee, inspection, add-ons: the dealer did not want this sale and will be paid for it anyway. The excise tax is the only line the state wrote. The rest are in lease buyout fees.

A specialist pays the excise tax to the MVA as part of titling the car and includes it in the loan. You never stand in line in Glen Burnie.

The rule. Maryland charges 6% vehicle excise tax on the buyout price, collected by the MVA when the car is titled in your name. There is no sales tax, no credit from the lease, and no way to title the car without paying it.

Why financing it matters

On a $20,000 residual the excise tax is $1,200. On $30,000 it is $1,800. Due the day the title moves. The payoff letter you are working from expires in days. The question is not whether you pay it. The question is whether it comes out of your checking account this week or sits inside the loan.

A big bank or a credit union lends against a book value and tells you the tax is yours. A dealer finances it at a rate with the dealer's compensation inside. A specialist finances residual, purchase-option fee and excise tax as one loan, pays the MVA, and sends you the tags. The homepage shows who refuses to finance what. Before anyone pulls your credit, get a written answer to one question: is the Maryland excise tax in the loan amount?

What to do with this

  1. Get the payoff letter from the leasing company. Note the price, the purchase-option fee, and the expiration date.
  2. Confirm the current excise tax rate and how it is assessed on a lease buyout with the Maryland MVA. The rate here is 6%; the MVA's figure is the one that counts.
  3. Multiply the buyout price by the rate. Add the MVA title, registration and lien charges. That is your real number.
  4. Decide who pays the tax, you or the loan, before the letter expires. Get it in writing.
  5. Any quote above residual plus fee plus excise tax plus MVA charges is somebody's margin. Ask which line it comes from.

If you are comparing with a neighbor across the river, Virginia's version of this tax is a different rate on a different form, and it is covered in Virginia lease buyout tax.

The short version

Maryland has no sales tax on vehicles, and that is not the same as no tax. The MVA charges a 6% vehicle excise tax on the purchase price when the car is titled in your name, and a lease buyout is a purchase. On a $20,000 residual that is $1,200, hypothetically, and nothing you paid during the lease reduces it.

Confirm the rate and the assessment with the MVA, put it in your number, and refuse any loan that leaves it for you to find in cash.

Quick answers

Is there sales tax on a lease buyout in Maryland?

Not sales tax. Maryland charges a 6% vehicle excise tax instead, collected by the MVA when the car is titled in your name. A lease buyout is a purchase, so the excise tax applies to the buyout price. Confirm the current rate with the MVA.

Didn't I already pay Maryland excise tax when the lease started?

Anything paid at the start of the lease was tied to the leasing company's title and your use of the car. The buyout puts a new title in your name, and the MVA assesses excise tax on that transfer. Ask the MVA directly if you believe a credit applies; do not take a dealer's word for it.

Can the excise tax be included in a lease buyout loan?

With the right lender, yes. Big banks and credit unions commonly lend against a book value and leave the tax to you. A specialist finances the residual, the purchase-option fee and the excise tax together and pays the MVA. Ask in writing before you apply.
